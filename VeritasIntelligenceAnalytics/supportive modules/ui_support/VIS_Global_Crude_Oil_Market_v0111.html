Market Dashboard

Compact VIS matrix: price tape, supply-demand, inventory, policy and risk transmission.

Updated
Price tape

Risk premium compressed

Brent and WTI have moved down from crisis-premium levels as the market focuses on returning flows through Hormuz.

Chokepoint risk

Hormuz still matters

Around one-fifth of global oil and LNG flows are tied to Hormuz; even easing risk can reverse quickly if shipping disruption returns.

Demand

High prices hit consumption

EIA now frames 2026 as a demand-down year, not a normal expansion year.

Supply

Supply recovery path is uneven

IEA expects a steep 2026 supply decline before a rebound, keeping the market dependent on geopolitical normalization.

Inventory

U.S. crude stocks drawn down

Commercial crude stocks fell to 418.2 million barrels after a large weekly draw, confirming tight near-term physical signals.

Refining

Refinery runs remain high

U.S. refinery inputs averaged 17.2 mb/d with utilization near 96.7%, amplifying crude demand during peak processing periods.

Macro link

Oil still feeds rates risk

Lower crude helps sentiment, but any renewed oil spike can quickly reprice inflation expectations and rates.

Format

Charts use VIS rules

Single-axis first, split-panel thinking, compact legends, no rainbow heatmaps, no three-axis charts.

Risk Scenario Matrix

Clear decision frame for a weekly oil market note.

Actionable
Base case

Flows normalize, prices range

Brent stabilizes around the high-70s / low-80s; inventories remain the key confirmation signal.

Stress case

Shipping risk returns

A renewed Hormuz disruption pushes risk premium back into crude and reawakens inflation/rates pressure.

Relief case

Risk premium fades further

Oil falls toward fundamentals, but weaker demand then becomes the equity and commodity-cycle risk.

Portfolio read-through

Do not chase only one narrative

Use energy exposure as hedge, but track demand weakness and rate-sensitive sectors at the same time.
