J P M O R G A N TSMC Margin test passed; On to 2026 with strong AI and N2 growth, with upside to GMs from pricing The strongest message from TSMC’s 2Q25 earnings conference was the resilience of GMs, despite unprecedentedly steep TWD appreciation and increased overseas Fab dilution. We expect 4Q25 to be the GM trough (due to UTR draw-down), with GMs likely to rebound in 2026 with price hikes, better N3 yield and accelerated productivity and cost-down measures. Demand trends for AI are strong with broader demand pickup (CSPs, sovereign AI and China demand) and CoWoS capacity likely to remain tight into 2026. While TSMC is cautious on 4Q25 (we now model 6% qoq decline in revenues), chances are that demand could shape up better if tariffs turn out to be less intense than feared. As such, we believe TSMC could eventually finish the year with low-mid 30% yoy growth in USD terms (JPMe 32%). We continue to see healthy growth in 2026, with a faster N2 ramp, price hikes from1Q26 and continued strength in N3 due to strong AI accelerator demand. We raise our FY25/ FY26 EPS estimates by 5/4% to reflect stronger growth prospects in AI and stay OW with a Dec-25 PT of NT$1,275. In the near term, we believe the stock may stay rangebound as the market is likely to focus on Sec 232 and specific country tariff outcomes. Once some clarity emerges on this front, we believe the stock should start pricing in strong growth and GM rebound in 2026, with some prospect of meaningful N2 outsourcing from Intel.

Increased utilization has helped, but strong cost-cutting measures have also contributed to keeping costs under check.
